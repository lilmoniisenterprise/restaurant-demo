Our story

Cooked by fire. Seasoned by the coast.

A neighbourhood restaurant on the Galveston seawall, built around an oak-fired hearth and the generosity of a long, shared table.

Chapter one

The hearth

Our kitchen has no gas line on the cooking line. Every morning the team lights the hearth with post oak, and by service the coals are glowing hot enough to char octopus in seconds and gentle enough to braise lamb for eight hours at the edges.

Cooking this way asks for attention. It rewards us with flavours you can't get any other way — smoke that settles into a crust, citrus caramelised against the grate, bread that blisters in under a minute.

Chapter two

The coast

We buy whole fish from small day-boat crews and break them down ourselves, so nothing goes to waste: bones become broth, trim becomes crudo, and the menu changes when the catch does.

  • Whole-animal, whole-fishNose-to-tail cooking keeps prices fair and waste low.
  • Seasonal by defaultOur menu is rewritten as Gulf Coast produce changes through the year.

Chapter three

The table

Ondessa is a place for long dinners. Dishes come out when they're ready and are meant to be passed around. Our 12-seat chef's counter puts you an arm's length from the fire, and the Tide Room hosts up to 24 guests for private celebrations.

Our commitments

Low waste

Whole-fish butchery, composting and house ferments.
